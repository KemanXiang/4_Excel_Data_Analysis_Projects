# Marketing Analysis --- Excel Data Analysis

This repository folder presents a collection of **nine marketing
analysis exercises and mini-projects** developed from my MBA coursework
in **MGT 209: Marketing Management** at the University of California,
Riverside. The projects use Excel and course-provided datasets to
examine marketing questions involving **consumer demand, marketing
effectiveness, pricing, segmentation, market structure, and sales
drivers**.

I organized and reconstructed these exercises as a compact record of my
quantitative marketing training. Rather than presenting them as
independent research studies, the collection shows how I have applied
statistical and analytical methods to marketing problems and how those
methods connect to managerial interpretation. Together, the projects
complement my broader research interests in **quantitative marketing,
marketing analytics, consumer and marketplace behavior, and data-driven
decision-making**.

## Selected Analytical Themes

### Marketing Metrics and Demand Drivers

The early exercises focus on translating marketing questions into
measurable relationships. The weather--sales exercise distinguishes
**association from causation**, while the advertising--traffic exercise
extends the analysis to regression-based prediction. The marketing-mix
exercise moves to a multivariate setting, examining advertising,
pricing, competitor behavior, and satisfaction simultaneously.

### Market Sizing, Demand Estimation, and Pricing

The diamond-pricing exercise compares alternative regression
specifications rather than assuming that one functional form must
describe the market. This introduced an important analytical principle
that continues to shape my work: **model specification should reflect
the structure of the relationship being studied, not simply the
availability of a statistical technique**.

### Segmentation and Targeting

Three exercises apply K-means clustering to different marketing
contexts: movie preferences, MBA programs, and toothpaste-related
consumer ratings. These projects emphasize the logic of **within-segment
similarity and between-segment differentiation**, while also
illustrating practical issues such as variable scaling, centroid
interpretation, Euclidean distance, and selection of the number of
clusters.

### Sales Drivers and Multivariate Marketing Analysis

The Group Assignment 1 analysis combines descriptive statistics,
correlation, and multiple regression to distinguish **bivariate
association from conditional relationships**. Standardized regression
coefficients are used when comparing predictors measured on different
scales, reinforcing the importance of careful interpretation rather than
relying only on raw coefficient magnitude.

### Innovation and Market Strategy

The VR gaming analysis complements the quantitative exercises with a
strategy-oriented application. It examines adoption through
diffusion-of-innovation concepts, considers the conditions under which
first-mover advantage may or may not persist, and evaluates launch
pricing in a technologically dynamic market.

## What This Collection Demonstrates

Across the nine exercises, the analytical progression moves from
descriptive analysis and correlation to regression, multivariate demand
analysis, clustering, segmentation, and strategic interpretation. The
collection demonstrates experience with:

-   translating marketing and managerial questions into analyzable
    variables;
-   conducting descriptive and exploratory analysis before modeling;
-   using correlation and regression to study demand and sales drivers;
-   comparing alternative regression specifications;
-   interpreting statistical significance, effect sizes, and model fit;
-   applying K-means clustering to consumer and market segmentation;
-   connecting quantitative outputs to marketing concepts such as
    demand, competition, targeting, pricing, adoption, and positioning;
    and
-   communicating analytical results in a form intended to support
    managerial reasoning.

## Research Motivation

These MBA exercises were originally designed as applied
marketing-management analyses rather than academic research projects.
Revisiting and organizing them has helped clarify the questions that now
motivate my interest in doctoral study: **why seemingly comparable
products and market offerings experience different outcomes, how
observable marketplace signals interact with heterogeneous preferences,
and how behavioral and market data can be used to explain and predict
demand**.

My broader goal is to build on this foundation through research in
**quantitative marketing and marketing analytics**, particularly work
involving consumer choice, marketplace dynamics, product performance,
pricing, and data-driven marketing decisions.

## Repository Structure

Each project folder contains the relevant **Excel analysis workbook**
and, where appropriate, the **course-provided CSV dataset** used for the
exercise. The workbooks generally preserve a consistent structure:

`Project Overview → Raw Data → Analysis / Results → Interpretation`

The Excel files are intended to make the analytical workflow
transparent: the original data are preserved, calculations and model
outputs are organized separately, and conclusions are linked back to the
marketing question.

> **Note:** These materials are course-based analytical exercises and
> are presented as evidence of methodological training and applied
> marketing analysis. They should not be interpreted as peer-reviewed
> research or as independent empirical studies.
